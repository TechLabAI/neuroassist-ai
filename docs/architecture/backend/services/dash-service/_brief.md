# DASH — Structured Brief (source of truth for Specs/Backlog/Scaffold)

> Module: **DASH — Dashboard e Notificações** · Service: `dash-service` · Package: `com.neuroassist`
> Project: NeuroAssist AI · Architecture: independent microservices (ADR-002 shared) · Messaging: disabled
> Generated by `/be-architect-vision neuroassist-ai DASH` · 2026-08-17

## 1. Canonical name and prefix
- **Prefix:** `DASH`
- **Canonical name:** `dash`
- **Service:** `dash-service` (Java 21 / Spring Boot backend module — the Backend-for-Frontend of the
  caregiver web panel)

## 2. Problem the module solves
A caregiver watching over a patient whose only channel to the world is a small facial movement needs **one
place** to do three things: see the honest timeline of what happened (alarms raised, confirmed `real` or
`falso_positivo`), tune how sensitive each command is, and — above all — **be told, immediately**, when the
patient asks for help. That information is spread across five services: HIST holds the history, PAC holds the
commands and their calibration, EVNT raises events and dispatches notifications, EDGE knows whether the
patient's device is alive, and ACS knows who the caregiver is and which patients they may see. Making the
caregiver's browser call five services, reconcile five auth models, and poll each one is fragile and leaks
the backend's shape into the UI. `dash-service` is the **Backend-for-Frontend**: a single, caregiver-scoped
boundary that composes those five sources into the panel's screens, relays EVNT's notifications to the
caregiver's browser in real time, and forwards the caregiver's actions (calibration edits, confirmations,
acknowledgements) to whichever service actually owns that truth. It is the front door of the platform for a
human — and it deliberately owns almost no domain state of its own.

## 3. Personas
- **Primary — Jamilly Amaral (family caregiver):** lives in this panel. She needs the emergency alarm to
  reach her screen the instant it happens, the routine notices to be visible but not alarming, the history
  to be readable at a glance, and a simple way to loosen or tighten a command that misfires. She acts; she
  does not want to know there are five services behind the glass.
- **Secondary — Danilo Amaral (technical conductor / clinic coordinator):** during onboarding, tuning and
  audits uses the same panel across one or more patients to read the audit trail, adjust calibration, and
  confirm device health. He needs the panel to respect who-can-see-which-patient and to be honest about
  device liveness ("is this offline dot real or is DASH stale?").
- **Indirect — Márcio Amaral (patient):** never touches the panel, but every screen exists to make his help
  requests reach a human fast and to make his care legible. The full-screen emergency alarm is, ultimately,
  his voice.

## 4. MVP scope (in)
- **Panel read-composition (BFF)**: aggregate, per caregiver and tenant, the data each screen needs by
  fanning out to HIST (history/audit timeline), PAC (patient + command catalog + current calibration), EVNT
  (event/alarm state and feedback verdicts) and EDGE (device online/offline liveness), returning
  panel-shaped view models — not raw upstream payloads.
- **Real-time notification delivery to the browser**: receive EVNT's `NotificationDispatch` calls
  (`full_screen_alarm` for `emergencia`, `discreet` for `rotina`), push them to the caregiver's connected
  panel over a **server-push channel (SSE)**, and report `delivered`/`acknowledged` back to EVNT — closing
  the dispatch lifecycle EVNT owns (ADR-005 local).
- **On-connect notification catch-up**: when a panel (re)connects, pull the caregiver's currently
  unacknowledged notifications from EVNT so nothing is missed across a disconnect — DASH keeps no durable
  notification store of its own (ADR-005 local).
- **Write-through caregiver actions**: forward calibration edits to PAC (the calibration authoring source,
  PAC ADR-004), event confirmations (`real`/`falso_positivo`) to EVNT (EVNT ADR-006), and notification
  acknowledgements to EVNT — DASH validates, authorizes and routes, but never becomes a second source of
  truth (ADR-006 local).
- **Caregiver scoping & session**: authenticate the caregiver (OAuth2/JWT aligned with ACS) and enforce
  tenant + caregiver↔patient visibility on every composed read and every proxied write, resolved via ACS.

## 5. Out of scope (MVP)
- Owning any operational/business state — DASH is not the system of record for events (EVNT), commands or
  calibration (PAC), caregivers/identity (ACS), history (HIST) or devices (EDGE). It composes and relays; it
  does not persist domain truth.
- Generating notifications or deciding urgency tier — EVNT owns notification orchestration and the
  deterministic `urgencia → tier` policy (EVNT ADR-005). DASH only *delivers* what EVNT dispatched.
- Authoring calibration values or confirming events on its own authority — those writes are proxied to their
  owners (PAC / EVNT).
- Any asynchronous broker / event bus (messaging disabled MVP) — EVNT→DASH dispatch and DASH↔upstream calls
  are synchronous REST; the browser channel is SSE/long-poll, not a broker.
- Out-of-band alerting (SMS, push, phone-call escalation of an unacknowledged emergency) — panel-only
  delivery in the MVP; escalation channels are deferred (a known risk inherited from EVNT ADR-005).
- Computer vision, gesture detection, device pairing/heartbeat internals and any video/image/landmark data —
  DASH renders derived state (e.g. an EDGE online/offline dot); it never touches biometric data.
- Analytics, reporting dashboards, cross-tenant/global consoles and long-term data retention — every view is
  scoped to a single `empresa` and computed live from the owner services.

## 6. Mandatory external integrations
- **HIST** (`hist-service`) — read: the history/audit timeline for a patient/subject, filtered by type,
  actor and time window; the data source for the "Histórico" screen.
- **PAC** (`pac-service`) — read: patient + command catalog and current calibration for the panel; write:
  proxied calibration edits (PAC is the authoring source, PAC ADR-004).
- **EVNT** (`evnt-service`) — inbound: receives `NotificationDispatch` calls from EVNT (DASH is EVNT's
  delivery target, EVNT ADR-005); outbound: pull unacknowledged notifications on connect, report
  delivery/acknowledgement, and proxy event confirmations (EVNT ADR-006).
- **EDGE** (`edge-service`) — read: device online/offline liveness for the patient, polled by DASH
  (established `DASH poll 15s`, ETag/304 liveness contract from EDGE); rendered as the device-status
  indicator.
- **ACS** (`acs-service`) — the authority for caregiver identity, authentication, roles and
  caregiver↔patient linkage; DASH resolves who-may-see-what against ACS on every request.
- **GWY / REG** — every DASH→upstream and EVNT→DASH call is REST routed via the Gateway and discovered via
  Eureka (independent microservices, ADR-002 shared). The caregiver's browser reaches DASH through GWY.

## 7. Hub / TechLab AI dependencies & inter-module contracts
- **TechLab AI services:** none. `ai.hasAi = false`; DASH is deterministic composition, delivery and
  proxying — no inference on any path (an emergency must never depend on a model).
- **Messaging:** disabled (`stack.messaging.enabled = false`) → EVNT→DASH notification dispatch and all
  DASH↔upstream traffic are **synchronous REST** (routed via GWY, discovered via REG). The browser receives
  notifications over a **server-push SSE channel** held open by DASH; the broker-based fan-out is deferred
  project-wide.
- **Consumes (by reference / read-through):** history (HIST), commands + calibration (PAC), events + notif
  dispatch state (EVNT), device liveness (EDGE), identity + linkage (ACS). **Produces/owns:** nothing
  durable in the domain — at most a live SSE session registry (in-memory) and, optionally, per-caregiver UI
  preferences (deferred). DASH is a **façade/relay**, not a source of truth.

## 8. Domain anchor event
- **"Notificação entregue ao cuidador" (notification delivered to caregiver)** — the moment an alarm/notice
  that EVNT dispatched actually reaches the caregiver's open panel and is rendered (and, ideally,
  acknowledged). This is DASH's reason to exist: it is the last, human-facing hop of the patient's help
  request. DASH does not own the notification's *existence* (EVNT does) — it owns its **delivery to a human**
  and the acknowledgement signal that flows back.
